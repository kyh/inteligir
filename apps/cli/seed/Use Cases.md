# Use Cases

Five kinds of work that fit notes with an agent beside them. Each one says what to write, what to ask the agent, and what to do next.

For how any of it works, see [[Getting Started|a618c388-0d20-4ebe-bcbd-55b9d59094ec]].

> [!TIP]
> Press ⌘K over the note you are working in and the agent starts from that note; type `@` to hand it others too. If you do not like what it changed, **Undo changes** on its reply takes it back.

---

## Meeting Notes

Notes from a meeting are worth keeping for what happens next.

**Write** rough notes while the meeting runs: who was there, what was said, what was decided. Do not tidy them.

**Ask the agent:**

> Pull the action items out of these notes into a checklist, one per line, with\
> an owner and a date. Leave a comment wherever the owner or the date is unclear.

**Then** tick items off as they get done, and link the next meeting's note to this one so the decisions stay connected.

---

## Research

Research goes stale when the findings and the sources drift apart. Keep them in one note and link outward.

**Write** the question in one sentence, then a note per source with what it says, linked from here.

**Ask the agent:**

> Read the linked notes and summarise where they agree and where they conflict.\
> Put the conflicts in a table with the source for each side.

**Then** watch **Related** in the Metadata tab: it surfaces notes that belong with this one even where you did not link them. When the research turns into a recommendation, ask for a one-page memo that leads with it.

---

## Drafting

A draft moves faster when you write the thinking and the agent takes the first pass at the prose.

**Write** an outline: the point you want to make, the sections, a few bullets under each.

**Ask the agent:**

> Draft the second section from my bullets, in the same plain tone as the first.\
> Leave the outline above it as it is.

**Then** read it through and comment where it went wrong — select the words and press ⌘⇧A — and ask the agent to answer your comments. If a pass makes things worse, **Undo changes** takes it back and keeps what you wrote since.

---

## Decisions

A decision note earns its keep months later, when someone asks why.

**Write** the decision itself in the first paragraph. Options and tradeoffs come after.

**Ask the agent:**

> Draft the tradeoff table for these options, then argue the strongest case\
> against the one I picked.

**Then** add a warning callout for what happens if the decision is wrong, and formula pills for the numbers that drove it — named, so a changed assumption moves everything built on it.

---

## Weekly Review

Daily notes catch the week as it happens; a review turns them into something you can act on.

**Write** a daily note each day with ⌘D: what you did, what got in the way, what comes next. A few lines is enough. Make a note named Daily in a folder named templates, and every new daily note starts from it.

**Ask the agent** at the end of the week, from a new note:

> Read my daily notes from this week and write a review: what got done, what\
> slipped and why, and three priorities for next week. Link each point to its day.

**Then** keep the reviews together in one folder, and read last month's before you plan the next.

---

## Combining Them

These are not separate modes. A meeting raises a question, research answers it, the answer becomes a decision, and the decision becomes a draft. The links between those notes are what make them worth more than the sum of their parts. Start one note, link the next, and let **Related** show you the shape you have been building.
